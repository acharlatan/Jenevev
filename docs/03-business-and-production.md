## Pricing model — example only

Formula: price = materials + allocated overhead + (artist hours × target hourly pay) + desired contribution. Actual taxes, payment fees, spoilage, consultation time, rework and unpaid time must also be considered. Never present this as a local market benchmark.

| Scenario | Materials | Allocated overhead | Time × illustrative rate | Desired contribution | Illustrative total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Simple | ₱100 | ₱50 | 1 hr × ₱250 = ₱250 | ₱100 | ₱500 |
| Detailed | ₱180 | ₱70 | 2 hr × ₱250 = ₱500 | ₱150 | ₱900 |
| Intricate | ₱250 | ₱100 | 3 hr × ₱250 = ₱750 | ₱250 | ₱1,350 |

For planning, contribution to fixed costs before paying artist time = price − variable materials and transaction costs. If monthly fixed costs were hypothetically ₱4,000 and the contribution per set after variable costs **and budgeted artist pay** were ₱200, break-even would be 20 sets per month (₱4,000 ÷ ₱200). Replace every input with real quotes, capacity and costs. Do not confuse revenue with profit or treat these numbers as demand forecasts.

Track date, service or practice type, product batch/expiry, amount consumed, time (including setup and cleanup), variable cost, overhead allocation, price if approved, and any quality issue. Avoid storing customer health details in an unsecured spreadsheet.

## Phased purchasing

The ₱2,500–₱4,000 weekly amount is an exploratory ceiling, not a commitment. First price and prioritize locally, check compatibility and product safety, and spread purchases across weeks. No unsupported item prices or total startup cost are assumed.

- **A — Training and safety:** credible training, safe workspace, ventilation assessment, hand hygiene and cleaning supplies, appropriate protective equipment, and manufacturer instructions. Learn safe product handling and local requirements first. Do not improvise sanitation or assume a UV product is suitable for everyone.
- **B — Core practice:** practice surfaces or tips, a small set of compatible professional products and basic tools appropriate to the actual training. Buy small quantities and track expiration.
- **C — Specialization:** fine brushes and a limited palette for original illustration; add specialty products only after technique and demand justify them.
- **D — Presentation:** daylight, neutral paper, a stable phone support and simple cards; upgrade lighting or packaging only if needed.
- **E — Expansion:** more stock, equipment or space only after actual capacity, repeat demand and financial records support it.
