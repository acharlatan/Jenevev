# Launch, content and measurement playbook

All activities are proposals. No launch, posts, audience growth, customer inquiries or results have occurred as part of this project.

## A phased launch

1. **Discover:** conduct the interviews in the brand document, map local competitors and assess training and operating needs. Decide which ideas are feasible. Exit: Jay chooses a narrow initial scope supported by evidence.
2. **Build the audience:** if Jay is comfortable, publish original drawings, honest practice and progress. Establish a consented way to receive messages. Exit: an active contact channel and genuine work to show.
3. **Prepare:** train and practice safely; confirm applicable local requirements, service scope, costs, appointment conditions, sanitation and aftercare. Create a small set of honest portfolio images. Exit: founder confirms readiness and can deliver consistently.
4. **Soft launch:** invite voluntary inquiries through a real channel; respond personally; record actual demand and feedback. Never call a request a confirmed booking until accepted. Exit: first experiences delivered safely and reviewed.
5. **Learn:** review feedback and time/cost records; update the offer and document only substantiated results. Expand only when evidence supports it.

## Ten short-form briefs and hooks

1. **Paper to possibility:** overhead sketch → close crop → concept. “What happens when a sketch gets really small?” CTA: save a detail.
2. **The one-line challenge:** draw three original motifs with one pen. “How much can one line say?” CTA: choose a favorite.
3. **Palette pull:** arrange paper swatches from a mood. “A color story for a day that feels like this.” CTA: name it.
4. **An imperfect first try:** show a real practice test and one lesson, with no invented outcome. “This one needed another try.” CTA: follow the process.
5. **Meet Jay:** hands drawing, voiceover about interests. “I’ve always liked making things.” CTA: say hello when a real channel exists.
6. **Close-up gallery:** slow pan over authentic work once available. “The best part is the detail you almost missed.” CTA: look closer.
7. **A or B:** two original sketches side by side. “Which one should I explore next?” CTA: vote.
8. **Tools, not magic:** simple approved materials and their purpose. “The little things behind a little idea.” CTA: ask a question.
9. **A mood in five shapes:** sketch five marks inspired by a word. “If this feeling had a pattern…” CTA: suggest a feeling.
10. **Building in public:** one honest decision and one next step. “Here’s where JENEVEV actually is today.” CTA: follow along.

Keep videos short enough for the story, use readable captions, disclose concept visuals, and film vertical 9:16 with safe space for platform controls. Avoid promises of services and trending audio without rights.

## Carousels and stories

**Five carousels:** (1) the idea behind the name and concept; (2) one sketch across three iterations; (3) a palette and its visual references; (4) a clearly labeled real practice diary; (5) questions we are asking before opening. Each begins with one concrete premise, uses readable image captions, and ends in one appropriate action.

**Five story interactions:** choose a palette; choose between two original marks; question box about trust; slider for subtle-to-expressive; vote on an actual next sketch. Never imply a poll is market-representative.

**Campaign themes:** “Small Canvas, Big Feeling” for expression; “The Open Sketchbook” for process and honesty; “Made to Be Seen Up Close” for details and eventual authentic work. A community participation campaign can invite votes on one real sketch, then show the chosen direction and credit contributors only with permission.

**Announcement sample:** “This is JENEVEV: a little space for art, ideas, and the details that make them personal. It’s early days, and I’m exploring what a new canvas could look like. Follow along as the sketchbook opens.”

**Founder introduction script:** “Hi, I’m Jay. I love painting, portraits, and drawing the details that make a character feel real. JENEVEV is a new creative idea I’m exploring, one small canvas at a time. There’s lots to learn, and I’m glad you’re here for the beginning.”

**Collection reveal script, for a real future release only:** “This started with [actual inspiration]. I explored [actual process], and the resulting collection is [confirmed name and availability]. Here’s a closer look at each piece. [State real date and conditions, or say availability is still to be announced.]”

## Measurement plan

The current static site sends no analytics and the contact draft is not an inquiry. There is no live dashboard, signup or booking data. Do not count local clicks as business outcomes. Before collecting analytics, select a privacy-conscious provider, document data handling and consent where required, and test event delivery. Never send contact text, names, email addresses, or health details as analytics properties.

| Event / measure | Definition | Source / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Website visit | A session under the selected provider's documented definition | Analytics, after consent/configuration |
| `gallery_view` | Gallery route viewed | Event; no assumption about interest quality |
| `design_filter_used` | User selects a gallery category | Event with category only |
| `moodboard_started` / `moodboard_completed` | First item selected / summary downloaded | Event; completion is not a lead |
| `service_view` | Service page viewed | Event; services currently unavailable |
| `inquiry_started` / `inquiry_submitted` | Actual form started / server accepted a valid submission | Only after a real integration exists; distinct from copied draft |
| `interest_signup` | Server accepts a consented signup | Only after actual list infrastructure exists |
| `booking_request_submitted` | Server accepts a valid request | Not a confirmed appointment |
| Confirmed appointments | Artist explicitly accepts a request | Manual booking log, not site clicks |
| Conversion | Accepted inquiries ÷ eligible sessions for a stated period | Same time window; show denominator and uncertainty |
| Repeat / referral | Actual returning customers / inquiries that voluntarily cite referral | Secure customer log, permissioned and minimal |

**Dashboard layout:** period and data freshness; visits and source; gallery engagement; inquiry starts and valid submissions; confirmed appointments; repeat/referral counts; experiment notes. Use “not connected” rather than zeros before measurement starts. Keep qualitative learning beside numbers.

## Experiment backlog

Each test records observation, hypothesis, change, held-constant factors, success measure, method, actual result, interpretation and next action. Results are pending for all five. Low traffic calls for exploratory comparisons and interviews, not claims of statistical significance.

1. **Positioning:** test whether the art-led description is understood versus a straightforward service description. Hold images and audience prompt constant; interview comprehension and interest; record objections. Next: revise words based on repeated confusion.
2. **Format:** compare process clips and finished work shown to a similar audience over comparable periods. Track qualified comments and saves per reach, with sample sizes. Next: make more of the format that produces useful questions, while noting confounding.
3. **Gallery organization:** observe five people finding a style using category versus a flat gallery. Record task completion and hesitation. Next: retain whichever makes finding ideas clearer.
4. **CTA:** once actual services exist, compare inquiry language with service exploration language in moderated sessions. Track intent and comprehension; do not compare a live booking promise before availability.
5. **Founder story:** interview viewers after founder-led versus brand-only content; record perceived trust and clarity. Use short exploratory comparisons, not definitive causation.
